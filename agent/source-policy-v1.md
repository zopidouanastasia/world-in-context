# World in Context — Source Registry v1.0

## Core rule
Source quality is claim-specific.

A source can be excellent for one class of claims and inappropriate for another.

## Verification pattern
Default for material stories:

1. Primary / official source
2. Genuinely independent reliable source
3. Supplementary source when complexity, controversy or impact justifies it

## Independence traps
Do NOT count as multiple independent confirmations:
- Reuters + websites republishing Reuters
- AP + websites republishing AP
- AMNA + websites republishing AMNA
- several official statements from institutions participating in the same policy chain
- several company-owned channels repeating the same company claim

## Stakeholder rule
Company, government and institutional statements are authoritative for:
- what they announced
- what decision they formally took
- what data they officially published within their remit

They are NOT automatically authoritative for:
- whether their policy/product succeeded
- comparative superiority
- causal impact
- disputed descriptions of events
- public adoption or real-world outcomes

## Data rule
For statistics always record:
- reference period
- publication date
- revision/preliminary/final status
- methodology caveats where material

## Forecast rule
Forecasts, scenarios, projections and model outputs must be explicitly labelled.

## Science rule
Priority:
peer-reviewed paper / authoritative assessment → independent scientific reporting → institutional or university press release.

## Breaking-news rule
Speed never overrides verification. For major disputed claims, single-source publication should be exceptional and clearly attributed.

## Editorial use
The registry is a priority map, not a whitelist that eliminates judgment.
A source not present in the registry may be used when relevant, but its role and reliability must be evaluated before it enters the final briefing.
